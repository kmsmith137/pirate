\section{1-d Kalman inpainting}
\label{sec:inpainting}

The Kalman detrender of \dtnotes{Time detrending algorithm 2: Kalman filter} sets its residual to zero at
masked samples.  Downstream, a masked sample therefore enters the dedispersion sums and the peak-finding
convolutions (\ddnotes{Peak-finding}) as a zero rather than as a noise sample, so the variance of a
peak-finder output depends on how much of its kernel lies on masked samples, and near masks it can be far
from the unmasked value.  {\em Inpainting} attacks this at the source: replace every masked sample by the
fitted baseline plus simulated noise, detrend again without a mask, and hand the second-pass residual
downstream, so that the peak-finder sees something that looks like detrended noise everywhere.

This section asks how well that works.  The data are pure noise and the mask is chosen adversarially:
how far can the variance of a peak-finder output stray from its unmasked value?  The answer turns on an
exact identity (\S\ref{sec:ip_identity}): the second pass cannot repair any variance the {\em first} pass
removed from valid samples, so every large deviation is a leverage effect of the masked detrender, and
for plain inpainting it is always a deficit.  Two refinements that enlarge the mask where the leverage is
high are then analyzed.  \S\ref{sec:ip_setup} sets up the problem, \S\ref{sec:ip_algorithms} states the
candidate algorithms, \S\ref{sec:ip_results} summarizes every result (saying which are theorems and which
are numerical), and Appendix \ref{app:ip} contains the proofs and details.

\subsection{Problem statement and notation}
\label{sec:ip_setup}

{\bf Setting.}  A one-dimensional timestream $d[t]$, $0 \le t < T$, with a boolean mask $m[t]$ ($1$ = valid)
and noise variance $\sigma^2$ independent of $t$.  Throughout, the data are pure noise,
$d[t] \sim {\cal N}(0,\sigma^2)$ i.i.d.: there is no baseline to subtract, so everything below is a property
of the mask and of the algorithm.  Both detrender passes are whole-timestream (``one-shot'') solves, i.e.\
the global penalized least-squares estimator of \dtnotes{The model}, not the fixed-lag estimator that
production uses (\S\ref{sec:ip_remarks} says what the fixed lag changes), and only samples far from the
ends of the timestream are considered, $\min(t, T-t) \gg \tau$.  The estimator does not depend on
$\sigma$, so we set $\sigma = 1$.

{\bf The Kalman detrender.}  The reader is assumed to know \dtnotes{Time detrending algorithm 2: Kalman
filter}; the objects used here are
\begin{align}
k &= \mbox{order of the integrated random walk ($k=2$ throughout)} \nn \\
\tau &= \mbox{smoothing timescale in samples ($\tau = 64$ throughout; \S\ref{sec:ip_tau} for other values)} \nn \\
\rho &= \tau^{2k} = \tau^4 \qquad \mbox{(penalty weight, $\rho = \sigma^2/q$)} \nn \\
M &= {\rm diag}(m), \qquad D = D_2 = \mbox{the $(T-2) \times T$ second-difference matrix} \nn \\
{\cal N}_m &= M + \rho D^TD \qquad \mbox{(normal matrix of the masked fit)} \nn \\
\hat f &= {\cal N}_m^{-1} M d \qquad \mbox{(maximum-likelihood baseline, defined at {\em every} $t$, masked or not)} \nn \\
r &= M(d - \hat f) \qquad \mbox{(the detrender's residual; zero at masked $t$)} \nn \\
\lambda[t] &= ({\cal N}_m^{-1})_{tt} = \partial \hat f[t]/\partial d[t] \qquad \mbox{(leverage, \dtnotes{Outputs})} \nn \\
R_m &= M - M {\cal N}_m^{-1} M \qquad \mbox{(residual operator: $r = R_m d$, and ${\rm Cov}(r) = R_m^2$ since $R_m$ is symmetric)} \nn \\
S_0 &= (I + \rho D^TD)^{-1} \qquad \mbox{(the {\em unmasked} smoother, ${\cal N}_m^{-1}$ at $m \equiv 1$)} \nn \\
h[s] &= (S_0)_{t,t+s} \qquad \mbox{(its impulse response, far from the ends)} .
\label{eq:ip_notation}
\end{align}
The unmasked smoother is a low-pass filter of unit mass, $\sum_s h[s] = 1$, and two of its numbers appear
in every result below:
\begin{equation}
h[0] = \frac{c_2}{\tau}, \qquad
\sum_s h[s]^2 = \frac{c_h}{\tau}, \qquad
c_2 = \frac{1}{2\sqrt2} = 0.354, \qquad
c_h = \frac{3}{8\sqrt2} = 0.265 .
\label{eq:ip_constants}
\end{equation}
$c_2$ is the flux-loss constant $c_k$ of \dtnotes{The model} at $k = 2$; both constants are derived in
\S\ref{sec:ip_constants}, where the discrete values at $\tau = 64$ are shown to agree with them to
$3 \times 10^{-5}$.

{\bf Peak-finding kernels.}  The reader is assumed to know \ddnotes{Peak-finding}.  For
$W_{\rm max} = 32$ there are $P = 3\log_2 W_{\rm max} + 1 = 16$ profiles: $p = 0$ is the single sample
$[1]$, and each level $j = 0,\ldots,4$ contributes three, the boxcar of width $2^{j+1}$ and the two
tapered kernels $[\tfrac12 \times 2^j,\ 1 \times 2^j,\ \tfrac12 \times 2^j]$ and
$[\tfrac12 \times 2^j,\ 1 \times 2^{j+1},\ \tfrac12 \times 2^j]$ (widths $3 \cdot 2^j$ and $4 \cdot 2^j$),
with $p = 3j + q$ for $q = 1,2,3$.  (The level is called $\lambda$ in \ddnotes{Peak-finding}; here
$\lambda$ is the leverage, so the level is $j$.)  The kernels are non-negative and unnormalized.  We write
\begin{align}
k_p &= \mbox{the kernel, as a vector; $k_{p,t}$ is the same vector placed at sample $t$} \nn \\
W_p &= \mbox{its support width (1 to 64)} \nn \\
W_{{\rm eff},p} &= (\textstyle\sum_i k_p[i])^2 / \|k_p\|^2 \qquad
   \mbox{($= W$ for a boxcar, $\tfrac83\,2^j$ and $\tfrac{18}{5}\,2^j$ for the tapered kernels)} \nn \\
y_p[t] &= \textstyle\sum_i k_p[i]\, x[t-i] \qquad \mbox{(the convolution of a residual $x$ with $k_p$)} .
\label{eq:ip_kernels}
\end{align}
Only variances are asked for, so the alignment convention of the convolution is immaterial.  The three
kernels quoted most often below are $p = 0$ (``p0''), the width-32 boxcar $p = 13$ (``box32'',
$W_{\rm eff} = 32$) and the widest tapered kernel $p = 15$ (``w64'', $W = 64$, $W_{\rm eff} = 57.6$).
The kernels are {\em not} narrow compared with the smoother: $W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ runs from $1/64$ (p0)
through $1/8$ (box8) to $0.5$ (box32) and $0.9$ (w64).  Several results below have a simple form to
first order in $W_{\rm eff}/\tau$; those leading-order formulas are quoted for orientation only, and an
exact number is given for every kernel.  At this $\tau$ the unmasked detrender by itself already removes
a large part of the wide kernels' variance: $U_p/\|k_p\|^2 = 0.78$ (box32) and $0.63$ (w64)
(Table \ref{tab:ip_Up}).

{\bf The quantities.}  For a fixed mask, averaging over the noise (and over any simulated noise the
algorithm draws),
\begin{align}
U_p &= {\rm Var}\big(y_p[t]\big) \mbox{ with no mask and } x = r \qquad \mbox{(independent of $t$; ``unmasked'')} \nn \\
V_p(t) &= {\rm Var}\big(y_p[t]\big) \mbox{ with the mask and } x = r \qquad \mbox{(the detrender as it stands)} \nn \\
V'_p(t) &= {\rm Var}\big(y_p[t]\big) \mbox{ with the mask and } x = r' \qquad \mbox{($r'$ = the inpainted residual defined next)} .
\label{eq:ip_quantities}
\end{align}
The question is how far $V'_p(t)/U_p$ can be from 1 when the mask and $(p,t)$ are chosen adversarially.
Since $k_{p,t}$ is a vector, everything is a quadratic form; e.g.\ $U_p = \|(I - S_0)k_{p,t}\|^2$.

Table \ref{tab:ip_Up} gives $U_p$ for all 16 kernels, both as defined and normalized by its value at
$\tau = \infty$.  At $\tau = \infty$ the smoother subtracts only a global straight line (the null space
of $D$), which has no effect on a compact kernel far from the ends, so $U_p(\tau{=}\infty) = \|k_p\|^2$,
and the normalized value $U_p/U_p(\tau{=}\infty)$ is the fraction of the kernel's variance that the
unmasked detrender leaves: the fractional impact of detrending on $U_p$, before any mask is applied.

\subsection{Algorithms}
\label{sec:ip_algorithms}

{\bf Algorithm 0 (plain inpainting).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Run the masked detrender once: $\hat f = {\cal N}_m^{-1}Md$ at every $t$, and $r = M(d - \hat f)$.
\item Draw $n'[t] \sim {\cal N}(0,\sigma^2)$ i.i.d., independent of $d$, and form
      $d' = Md + (I - M)(\hat f + n')$: valid samples keep their data, masked samples get the baseline plus
      simulated noise.
\item Run the detrender on $d'$ with {\em no} mask: $r' = (I - S_0)\,d'$.
\item Convolve with the peak-finding kernels, $y'_p = k_p * r'$.
\end{enumerate}

{\bf Algorithm A (leverage-enlarged inpainting).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item Run the masked detrender once with the original mask: $\hat f = {\cal N}_m^{-1}Md$ at every $t$,
      $r = M(d - \hat f)$, and the leverage $\lambda[t] = ({\cal N}_m^{-1})_{tt}$ at every valid $t$.
\item Enlarge the mask by dropping valid samples whose leverage is too high:
\begin{equation}
m_2[t] = m[t] \;\wedge\; \big(\lambda[t] \le \lambda_{\rm thr}\big), \qquad
\lambda_{\rm thr} = \mu\, \frac{c_2}{\tau}, \qquad M_2 = {\rm diag}(m_2) \le M .
\label{eq:ip_enlarge}
\end{equation}
      The threshold is a multiple $\mu$ of the interior leverage $h[0] = c_2/\tau$: $\mu = 1$ is the
      leverage in an unmasked stretch, $\mu = 4$ the leverage at the edge of a semi-infinite gap (to
      $1\%$: $3.96$ at $\tau = 64$), and $\mu = \infty$ drops nothing.
\item Draw $n'[t] \sim {\cal N}(0,\sigma^2)$ i.i.d., independent of $d$, and form
      $d' = M_2 d + (I - M_2)(\hat f + n')$: samples valid in $m_2$ keep their data; samples masked in
      $m_2$, whether originally masked or dropped in step 2, get the {\em first-pass} baseline plus
      simulated noise.
\item Run the detrender on $d'$ with {\em no} mask: $r' = (I - S_0)\,d'$.
\item Convolve with the peak-finding kernels, $y'_p = k_p * r'$.
\end{enumerate}
With $\mu = \infty$ this is algorithm 0.

{\bf Algorithm C (A with the dropped residuals removed).}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[1--3.] As in algorithm A.
\item[4.] Let $u = (M - M_2)\,r$ be the first-pass residual on the samples dropped in step 2 (zero
      elsewhere).  Run the detrender on $d'$ with no mask, and subtract the smoothed $u$:
\begin{equation}
r'_C = (I - S_0)\,d' - S_0\, u .
\label{eq:ip_algC}
\end{equation}
      This needs only one unmasked detrender run, not two.\footnote{Since $(I - S_0)u - u = -S_0 u$,
      (\ref{eq:ip_algC}) can be rewritten as $r'_C = (I - S_0)(d' + u) - u$: run the unmasked detrender
      once on $d' + u$, then subtract $u$ from its residual, which touches only the dropped samples.  So
      algorithm C costs the same as algorithm A, and the detrender need not process two data vectors.
      The input $d' + u$ has a simple meaning too: on a dropped sample $\hat f + n' + r = d + n'$, so
      $d' + u = Md + (I - M)(\hat f + n') + (M - M_2)\,n'$ is algorithm 0's inpainted data with fresh
      simulated noise added on the dropped samples; subtracting $u$ afterwards removes their first-pass
      residual from the output.  Equation (\ref{eq:ip_algC}) remains the definition because it makes the
      identity (\ref{eq:ip_identities}) for C immediate.}
\item[5.] Convolve with the peak-finding kernels, $y'_p = k_p * r'_C$.
\end{enumerate}

(A fourth variant, algorithm B, re-runs the first pass with the enlarged mask and inpaints with the new
baseline.  It is strictly worse than A and C; see \S\ref{sec:ip_remarks}.)

\subsection{Summary of results}
\label{sec:ip_results}

Numbers are for $\tau = 64$; each is labelled as a theorem (proved below) or as
numerical (exact linear algebra for a given mask, with adversarial masks found by simulated annealing,
so ``worst found'' rather than ``worst possible'').  Expressions to first order in $W_{\rm eff}/\tau$
are quoted for orientation only (\S\ref{sec:ip_setup}); every quoted number is exact for the kernel named.

\begin{enumerate}

\item {\bf Exact identities} (theorem, \S\ref{sec:ip_identity}).  With $r$ the first-pass residual,
\begin{align}
\mbox{algorithm 0:}\quad r' &= r + (I - S_0)(I - M)\,n' , \nn \\
\mbox{algorithm A:}\quad r' &= M_2 r + S_0 (M - M_2)\, r + (I - S_0)(I - M_2)\,n' , \nn \\
\mbox{algorithm C:}\quad r'_C &= M_2 r + (I - S_0)(I - M_2)\,n' .
\label{eq:ip_identities}
\end{align}
The second pass returns the first-pass residual on every sample it kept, plus high-passed simulated
noise on the samples it inpainted.  Inpainting therefore restores the masked samples' own variance and
{\em cannot repair a variance deficit on valid samples}: whatever the masked first pass removed from them
through its leverage stays removed.  The variances follow exactly, e.g.\ for algorithm 0
\begin{equation}
V'_p(t) = \|R_m k\|^2 + \|(I - M)(k - S_0 k)\|^2, \qquad
V_p(t) = \|R_m k\|^2, \qquad U_p = \|(I - S_0)k\|^2 \qquad (k = k_{p,t}) .
\label{eq:ip_var0}
\end{equation}

\item {\bf Upper side} (theorem, \S\ref{sec:ip_upper}).  For algorithms 0 and C and every mask,
$V'_p/U_p \le B_{1,p}/U_p$ with $B_{1,p} = \sum_s \max(k_s^2, (k_s - (S_0k)_s)^2)$: $1.404$ for box32,
$1.804$ for w64, $1.011$ for p0; for A the bound adds $\|S_0k\|^2/U_p$ ($0.17$, $0.37$, $0.004$).  The
leading-order form $1 + 2c_2 W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ ($1.354$, $1.636$) no longer even bounds the exact one.
The bound is not approached: the largest value found by annealing (algorithm 0) is
$1 + 0.050\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau = 1.025$ (box32), $1 + 0.056\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau = 1.051$ (w64) and $1.0003$
(p0), from a sparse comb around the kernel plus a masked annulus.  The upper side of plain inpainting is
thus a few percent, an order of magnitude below the bound.  With enlargement it is larger: algorithm C
exceeds 1 by $12\%$ (box32) and $19\%$ (w64) for a kernel on a kept run beside enlarged samples
(\S\ref{sec:ip_bterm}; confirmed by Monte Carlo), and a dedicated maximization (\S\ref{sec:ip_fom}) finds
larger values at small $\mu$: $V'/U - 1$ up to $0.083$ / $0.18$ at $\mu = 2$ and $0.136$ / $0.28$ at
$\mu = 1.2$ for box32 / w64, still inside the bound.

\item {\bf Lower side of algorithm 0} (numerical, \S\ref{sec:ip_lower0}).  With at most $B$ masked samples
near the kernel (``window'' = the whole $\pm 24\tau$ search window, $3073$ samples), the worst found is

Up to $B \approx 1.5\tau$ ($2\tau$ for w64) the worst case is the kernel exactly filling a gap of its own
width, $V'/U = 0.871$ (box32), $0.788$ (w64), $0.996$ (p0) (leading order $1 - c_h W_{\rm eff}/\tau$:

of $B/2$ on each side, whose mean the first pass absorbs.  Half the leverage ($V'/U = 0.56$) is reached at
two-sided gaps of $117$ samples around the 32-sample support, and a kernel simply touching a free gap is
already there ($V'/U = 0.60$ for box32, $0.57$ for w64, i.e.\ a $40$--$43\%$ loss; $0.86$ for box8,
$0.98$ for p0).  The floors are $0.129$ (box32; $c_h W/\tau = 0.133$), $0.295$ (w64: a new structure, the
32-sample unit-weight core left valid with the shoulders masked; $0.371$ if the whole support is left
valid) and $0.0042$ (p0).  Random masks that leave every valid run long compared with $\tau$ are
harmless, where ``long'' now means $\gtrsim 4\tau$.

\item {\bf Lower side with enlargement.}  For algorithm C the leverage threshold gives a rigorous bound
(theorem, \S\ref{sec:ip_lowerC}), for every mask,
\begin{equation}
\frac{V'_p(t)}{U_p} \;\ge\; {\rm LB}_p(\mu)
   \;=\; 1 - \big[\, 2(\mu - 1)\, c_2 + c_h \,\big]\frac{W_{{\rm eff},p}}{\tau}
   + O\!\left(\frac{W^2}{\tau^2}\right) ,
\label{eq:ip_LB}
\end{equation}
where ${\rm LB}_p(\mu)$ is the exact expression (\ref{eq:ip_LB_exact}) and the expansion is for
orientation only (it is off by up to $0.12$ and goes negative for box32 at $\mu \ge 3.5$, w64 at
$\mu \ge 2.4$).  Exactly, at $\mu = 1.2$, $1.5$, $2$, $3$, $4$, ${\rm LB}$ is $0.791$, $0.688$,
$0.533$, $0.281$, $0.109$ for box32 and $0.608$, $0.435$, $0.211$, $0.003$, $0$ for w64, and the bound vanishes
identically once $\mu c_2 W_{\rm eff}/\tau \ge 1$ (w64 at $\mu \ge 3.14$, box32 at $\mu \ge 5.66$).  It
is weak: the worst masks found are $0.08$--$0.42$ above it.  For algorithm A there is no bound of this
form: the term $S_0(M - M_2)r$ in (\ref{eq:ip_identities}) costs about $0.5\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ ($0.25$
for box32, $0.35$ for w64) for a kernel on a kept run between enlarged zones, independently of $\mu$ up to
$\mu \approx 2$ (\S\ref{sec:ip_bterm}).  A nevertheless stays above ${\rm LB}(\mu)$ in every run (by
$\ge 0.05$): the $b$-term is present at full strength, but on those masks the $a$-part
$\|R_m M_2 k\|^2$ is far from the extremal value the bound assumes.  Its worst cases are numerical only.
Table \ref{tab:ip_summary} gives the worst found for both, with the bound and the cost of the
enlargement.

\item {\bf Cost of the enlargement} (numerical, \S\ref{sec:ip_overmask}).  On an isolated gap the rule
masks extra samples on each side, out to where the leverage falls below threshold: for a $2048$-sample
gap ($32\tau$), $72$/$50$/$31$/$10$/$0$ samples per side at $\mu = 1.2$/$1.5$/$2$/$3$/$4$ (a free end:
$72$/$51$/$33$/$13$/$0$), nothing for gaps up to $128$ once $\mu \ge 2$, up to $512$ once $\mu \ge 3$,
and nothing at all for $\mu \ge 4$.  A region of valid density $n$ has leverage
$\approx n^{-3/4}\,c_2/\tau$ (to $0.3\%$ in the mean), so everything below density $\mu^{-4/3}$ is masked
wholesale ($0.78$/$0.58$/$0.40$/$0.23$/$0.16$).  On a random mask of gaps with lengths log-uniform in
$[1, 512]$ covering $10\%$ of the samples, $6.3$/$2.8$/$0.9$/$0.02$/$0.01\%$ of the valid samples are lost
($1.5$/$0.8$/$0.4$/$0.08$/$0\%$ for lengths in $[1, 4096]$).

\item {\bf Verification} (\S\ref{sec:ip_numerics}).  Every formula was checked against a Monte Carlo of
the literal procedure at $\tau = 64$: for algorithm 0, $143632$ (kernel, position) pairs over eleven
masks, with normally distributed pulls; for A and C, $128$ (kernel, variant) pairs on the four worst
masks at $\mu = 2$.

\end{enumerate}

{\bf Bottom line.}  At $\tau = 64$ with $W_{\rm max} = 32$, plain inpainting no longer even fixes the
problem it was designed for: a kernel filling a gap of its own width comes back to $0.87$ (box32) or
$0.79$ (w64), a $13$--$21\%$ deficit, and a kernel beside a long gap is at $0.60$.  An adversary needs only
a few hundred masked samples ($B = 256$, gaps of $2\tau$) to halve the variance of box32, and about
$2000$ to drive it to $13\%$.  A per-sample leverage threshold lifts the floor, but not to a useful
level for the wide kernels.  With $\mu = 3$--$4$ it costs nothing on benign masks and raises the floor
from $0.13$/$0.30$ to $0.31$--$0.52$ (box32) / $0.36$--$0.39$ (w64); with $\mu = 2$ it costs $31$ samples
per long-gap edge and all data below density $0.4$, and gives $0.70$ (A) or $0.78$ (C) for box32 and
$0.59$/$0.63$ for w64; below $\mu \approx 1.5$ nothing more is gained (C is at the gap-filling floor
$0.871$/$0.788$, A saturates at $0.84$/$0.79$ because of its $S_0(M-M_2)r$ term) while the cost doubles.
No threshold reaches $0.90$ for box32 or w64.  Across the 16 kernels (\S\ref{sec:ip_lowerC}), a
guarantee of $0.95$ from algorithm C is available only for $W \le 8$ (box8 at $\mu \le 2$; w12 only at
$\mu \le 1.2$), and $0.90$ up to $W = 16$ at $\mu \le 2$ (box16/w16 at $\mu \le 1.9$/$2.1$; w24, w32 and
w48 only at $\mu \le 1.7$, $1.6$ and $1.4$); for A subtract the $b$-term, so $0.95$ needs $W \le 8$ at
$\mu \lesssim 1.5$ and $0.90$ holds for $W \le 12$ at $\mu \le 2$.  The deficits are no longer small
multiples of $W_{\rm eff}/\tau$: they are order one, and even in those units the coefficients of the
$\tau = 512$ baseline survive only for box32 (to $10$--$20\%$), not for w64 (\S\ref{sec:ip_tau}).  The
route to a real guarantee is the exact per-$(p,t)$ variance, used to renormalize rather than to mask
(\S\ref{sec:ip_remarks}); and the ratio $W_{\rm max}/\tau$ itself deserves scrutiny, since the unmasked
detrender already removes $22$--$37\%$ of the wide kernels' variance ($U_p/\|k_p\|^2 = 0.78$/$0.63$)
before any mask is applied.  These are pointwise statements; under the chunk-averaged figure of merit of
\S\ref{ssec:weaker_fom} the lower side shrinks by an order of magnitude and the ranking of A and C reverses.

\subsection{A weaker figure of merit?}
\label{ssec:weaker_fom}

Given an inpainting algorithm, consider two figures of merit:
\begin{align}
X_{\rm hi}(p) &\equiv \max_{m,t} \mbox{ReLU} \left( \frac{V'_p(t)}{U_p} - 1 \right) \nn \\
X_{\rm lo}(p) &\equiv \max_{m,t} \mbox{ReLU} \left( 1 -  \frac{V'_p(t)}{U_p} \right)
\label{eq:Xhl_def}
\end{align}
where ${\rm ReLU}(x) \equiv \max(x,0)$ as usual.
Intuitively, $X_{\rm hi}$ tells us how much $V'_p$ can ``overshoot'' $U_p$, and $X_{\rm lo}$ tells us how much $V'_p$ can ``undershoot'' $U_p$, if the mask $m$ and time $t$ are chosen adversarially.

Previous work has explored the values of $(X_{\rm hi}, X_{\rm lo})$ for inpainting algorithms 0,A,B,C, and for varying choices of the $\mu$-parameter. So far, we're finding that it's (comparatively) easier to make $X_{\rm hi}$ small, and harder to make $X_{\rm lo}$ small. 

Next, I'd like to explore the following question: suppose we leave the definition of $X_{\rm hi}$ unchanged, but weaken the definition of $X_{\rm lo}$ as follows:
\begin{equation}
\tilde X_{\rm lo}(p) \equiv \max_{m,t} \mbox{ReLU} \left( 1 - \frac{1}{T_cU_p} \sum_{t'=t}^{t+T_c-1} V'_p(t') \right)
\end{equation}
That is, instead of evaluating $V'_p$ at an adversarially chosen time $t$, we average it over an adversarially chosen time window $[t,t+T_c)$, where the ``chunk size'' $T_c=1024$.

Please analogously explore values of $(X_{\rm hi}, \tilde X_{\rm lo})$ for inpainting algorithms 0,A,C, and for varying choices of the $\mu$-parameter.

{\bf Results.}  Table \ref{tab:ip_fom} gives the answer for the two widest kernels; every
entry is the best found by simulated annealing (exact for the mask exhibited, a lower bound on the true
maximum), with $\tilde X_{\rm lo}$ seeded by an exact scan over periodic masks.  All narrow kernels are
unproblematic under either figure: for $W \le 8$ every entry of the corresponding table is below $0.03$
for every algorithm and $\mu$ (box8: $X_{\rm hi} \le 0.006$ for algorithms 0 and A, $\le 0.033$ for C;
$\tilde X_{\rm lo} \le 0.029$), and p0 is below $0.005$ throughout.  The six-kernel version is Table
\ref{tab:ip_fom_full}, and the evaluator, the searches, the worst-mask structures, the Monte Carlo checks
and the caveats are in \S\ref{sec:ip_fom}.

\subsection{More notation, and the constants of the smoother}
\label{sec:ip_constants}

{\bf The hat matrix and the correlation length.}  Two more objects from \dtnotes{Time detrending
algorithm 2: Kalman filter} are used in the proofs:
\begin{align}
L_m &= M {\cal N}_m^{-1} M \qquad \mbox{(masked hat matrix, so that $R_m = M - L_m$)} \nn \\
\ell &= \sqrt2\,\tau = 90.5 \qquad \mbox{(correlation length: entries of ${\cal N}_m^{-1}$ decay as $e^{-|t-t'|/\ell}$)} .
\label{eq:ip_notation2}
\end{align}
$L_m$ is the hat matrix $H = {\cal N}_m^{-1}M$ of \dtnotes{Outputs} restricted to the valid samples, made
symmetric, and $\lambda[t] = (L_m)_{tt}$ at valid $t$.  ${\cal N}_m$ is positive definite as soon as two
samples are valid.  The $r_{\rm min}$ mask expansion of \dtnotes{Outputs} is ignored throughout; it fires
only at the start of a stream.

{\bf The unmasked smoother.}  $S_0$ reproduces cubics: its normal equations $(I + \rho D^TD)f = d$ hold
with equality for any $f$ with vanishing fourth difference.  In particular $\sum_s h[s] = 1$.
Far from the ends, $S_0$ is Toeplitz with spectrum $s(\omega) = 1/(1 + \rho\,(2 - 2\cos\omega)^2)$, which
for $\omega\tau \ll 1$ is $1/(1 + (\omega\tau)^4)$.  Then
$h[0] = \int \frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\, s = \frac{1}{\tau}\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{\pi}{\sqrt2} = \frac{c_2}{\tau}$ and
$\sum_s h[s]^2 = \int \frac{d\omega}{2\pi}\, s^2 = \frac{1}{\tau}\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{3\pi}{4\sqrt2} = \frac{c_h}{\tau}$,
which is (\ref{eq:ip_constants}); the discrete values at $\tau = 64$ agree to $3 \times 10^{-5}$ ($h[0]$)
and $1 \times 10^{-5}$ ($\sum h^2$), and at $\tau = 512$ to $5 \times 10^{-7}$.  In units of $\ell$ the
shape of $h$ is scale-invariant: it is positive and decreasing out to $|s| = 2.36\,\ell$ ($3.3\tau$),
with $h(\ell)/h[0] = 0.51$ and $h(2\ell)/h[0] = 0.067$, beyond which comes a first negative lobe of depth
$4.3\%$ of $h[0]$ (at $3.1\,\ell$) and integrated mass $-0.07$ of the total.  For a kernel narrow compared
with $\ell$ the smoothed kernel is flat across the support, $(S_0 k)_s \approx h[0]\sum k$; at
$\tau = 64$ it is flat to $2.4\%$ (box32) and $8.7\%$ (w64), and $h[0]\sum k$ overestimates its maximum
by $1\%$ and $2.7\%$.  To leading order in $W/\tau$,
\begin{equation}
\langle k, S_0 k\rangle \approx \frac{c_2 (\sum k)^2}{\tau}, \qquad
\|S_0 k\|^2 \approx \frac{c_h (\sum k)^2}{\tau}, \qquad
U_p = \|k\|^2 - 2\langle k, S_0 k\rangle + \|S_0 k\|^2 \approx \|k\|^2\Big(1 - 0.442\,\frac{W_{{\rm eff},p}}{\tau}\Big) .
\label{eq:ip_Up}
\end{equation}
This is why $W_{\rm eff} = (\sum k)^2/\|k\|^2$, and not the support width, is the parameter that
appears everywhere.  The leading-order $U_p$ is accurate to $0.1\%$ up to box16 and is $0.7\%$ (box32),
$1.7\%$ (w48) and $4.3\%$ (w64) low (the $O(W^2/\tau^2)$ term is positive); the exact $U_p$ of every
kernel, and its ratio to $\|k\|^2$, are in Table \ref{tab:ip_Up}.  The kernel constants that enter
(\ref{eq:ip_Up}) are:

\subsection{The exact identities}
\label{sec:ip_identity}

{\bf Algorithm 0.}  Since $Md = M\hat f + r$, the inpainted data are $d' = \hat f + r + (I - M)n'$.  The
normal equations ${\cal N}_m \hat f = Md$ say $\rho D^TD \hat f = Md - M\hat f = r$.  And the unmasked
smoother satisfies $I - S_0 = S_0(S_0^{-1} - I) = \rho\, S_0 D^TD$.  Hence
\begin{equation}
(I - S_0)\hat f = \rho\, S_0 D^TD \hat f = S_0\, r, \qquad
(I - S_0)(\hat f + r) = S_0 r + r - S_0 r = r ,
\end{equation}
and $r' = (I - S_0)d' = r + (I - S_0)(I - M)n'$.  The proof uses exactly: the same $\rho$ (same $\tau$) and
the same $D$ (same $k$ and the same diffuse treatment of the ends) in both passes, whole-timestream
solves so that the normal equations hold, masked samples filled with the first-pass $\hat f$ at those
samples (plus anything independent), and an unmasked second pass.  Nothing is assumed about $d$ or $n'$;
the identity is deterministic.  Numerically it holds to $3 \times 10^{-13}$; with $\rho$ changed by a
factor 2 in the second pass it fails at the $5\%$ level.

{\bf Algorithm A.}  $M_2 \le M$ gives $M_2 r = M_2(d - \hat f)$, so $M_2 d = M_2 \hat f + M_2 r$ and
$d' = \hat f + M_2 r + (I - M_2)n'$.  Then
\begin{equation}
(I - S_0)(\hat f + M_2 r) = S_0 r + M_2 r - S_0 M_2 r = M_2 r + S_0 (M - M_2)\, r
\end{equation}
using $r = Mr$.  The middle term is a smoothed copy of the residuals at the samples the enlargement
dropped: their data are gone from $d'$, but their imprint on $\hat f$ is not.

{\bf Algorithm C} subtracts exactly that term, $S_0 u$ with $u = (M - M_2)r$, leaving
$r'_C = M_2 r + (I - S_0)(I - M_2)n'$.

{\bf Algorithm B} (first pass re-run with $m_2$, baseline $\hat f_2 = {\cal N}_{m_2}^{-1}M_2 d$, residual
$r_2 = M_2(d - \hat f_2)$) is algorithm 0 with $m \rightarrow m_2$, so $r' = r_2 + (I - S_0)(I - M_2)n'$.

{\bf Variances.}  $r = R_m d$ with $R_m = M - M{\cal N}_m^{-1}M$ symmetric, so ${\rm Cov}(r) = R_m^2$; $n'$
is independent of $r$; and $S_0$ is symmetric.  For a kernel vector $k = k_{p,t}$,
\begin{align}
\mbox{0:}\quad V'_p(t) &= \|R_m k\|^2 + \|(I - M)(k - S_0 k)\|^2 , \nn \\
\mbox{A:}\quad V'_p(t) &= \|R_m \tilde k\|^2 + \|(I - M_2)(k - S_0 k)\|^2 , \qquad
  \tilde k = M_2 k + (M - M_2) S_0 k , \nn \\
\mbox{C:}\quad V'_p(t) &= \|R_m M_2 k\|^2 + \|(I - M_2)(k - S_0 k)\|^2 , \nn \\
\mbox{B:}\quad V'_p(t) &= \|R_{m_2} k\|^2 + \|(I - M_2)(k - S_0 k)\|^2 ,
\label{eq:ip_variances}
\end{align}
with $U_p = \|(I - S_0)k\|^2$ and $V_p(t) = \|R_m k\|^2$.  The first term in each line is the
contribution of the first-pass residual; the second is the simulated noise, high-passed and restricted to
the inpainted samples.

\subsection{Upper bounds}
\label{sec:ip_upper}

{\bf Lemma.}  $0 \le L_m \le M$, hence $0 \le R_m \le M \le I$ and $R_m^2 \le R_m$.  {\em Proof.}
$L_m = M{\cal N}_m^{-1}M \ge 0$ is clear.  For $w = Mw$, the variational form
$w^T{\cal N}_m^{-1}w = \max_v \big(2v^Tw - v^T{\cal N}_m v\big)$ and ${\cal N}_m \ge M$ give
$w^T{\cal N}_m^{-1}w \le \max_v \big(2v^Tw - v^TMv\big) = \max_u \big(2u^Tw - u^Tu\big) = w^Tw$ with
$u = Mv$.  So $v^TL_mv \le v^TMv$ for every $v$.  (At $v = e_t$ this is the $\lambda[t] \le 1$ of
\dtnotes{Outputs}.)  $\square$

{\bf Algorithm 0.}  By the lemma $\|R_m k\|^2 = k^TR_m^2k \le k^TMk = \|Mk\|^2$, so
\begin{align}
V'_p(t) &\le \|Mk\|^2 + \|(I - M)(k - S_0 k)\|^2 = \sum_s \Big[ m_s k_s^2 + (1 - m_s)\big(k_s - (S_0k)_s\big)^2 \Big] \nn \\
   &\le \sum_s \max\!\Big( k_s^2,\ \big(k_s - (S_0k)_s\big)^2 \Big) =: B_{1,p} .
\label{eq:ip_B1}
\end{align}
On the support $0 \le (S_0k)_s \le 0.258 \le k_s < 2k_s$ (the maximum of $(S_0k)_s$ over all $s$ lies on
the support for every kernel, and is $0.258$ for w64 against $k_s \ge \tfrac12$: the condition holds for
all 16 kernels, with little room to spare), so the maximum is $k_s^2$ there; off the support it is
$(S_0k)_s^2$.  Hence $B_{1,p} \le \|k\|^2 + \|S_0k\|^2$, and with (\ref{eq:ip_Up})
\begin{equation}
\frac{V'_p(t)}{U_p} \;\le\; \frac{B_{1,p}}{U_p} \;\le\; 1 + \frac{2\langle k, S_0k\rangle}{U_p} \;=\; 1 + 2c_2\,\frac{W_{\rm eff}}{\tau} + O\!\Big(\frac{W^2}{\tau^2}\Big) .
\label{eq:ip_upper}
\end{equation}
Exactly, $B_{1,p}/U_p = 1.404$ (box32), $1.804$ (w64), $1.011$ (p0); the loosened form
$1 + 2\langle k, S_0k\rangle/U_p$ is $1.443$, $1.964$, $1.011$; and the leading-order expression
$1 + 2c_2W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ is $1.354$, $1.636$, $1.011$, {\em below} the exact bound by $4$--$17\%$ (the
$O(W^2/\tau^2)$ term is $+0.05$ for box32 and $+0.17$ for w64), so at $\tau = 64$ only the exact form is
the content of the theorem.  A tighter bound, $1 + c_2 W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ at leading order, follows from
$\|R_mk\|^2 \le k^TR_mk = \|Mk\|^2 - (Mk)^T{\cal N}_m^{-1}(Mk)$ and ${\cal N}_m^{-1} \ge S_0$ (more mask,
larger inverse), {\em provided} the resulting mask-dependent expression
$B_2 = \max_m [\|Mk\|^2 - (Mk)^TS_0(Mk) + \|(I - M)(k - S_0k)\|^2]$ is attained by the all-valid support.
At $\tau = 64$ that proviso is false for the ten kernels of width $\ge 12$: masking $1$--$24$ support
samples (the half-weight shoulders of the tapered kernels, edge samples of the boxcars) raises the
expression by up to $10^{-2}$ in the ratio.  The maximum found by local search over support masks is
$B_2/U_p = 1.184$ (box32), $1.352$ (w64), $1.0055$ (p0), a numerical statement only.  Neither bound is
approachable: saturating them needs zero leverage on the kernel and every sample outside the support
masked, and the second makes the support an island, whose mean mode has leverage near 1.

{\bf Algorithms A and C.}  For C the first term is $\|R_m M_2 k\|^2 \le \|M_2 k\|^2$ and the same
argument gives (\ref{eq:ip_upper}) unchanged.  For A, $\|R_m\tilde k\|^2 \le \|\tilde k\|^2 =
\|M_2k\|^2 + \|(M - M_2)S_0k\|^2 \le \|M_2k\|^2 + \|S_0k\|^2$ (the two pieces of $\tilde k$ have disjoint
supports), which adds $\|S_0k\|^2/U_p$ to the bound: $0.17$ (box32), $0.37$ (w64), $0.004$ (p0), i.e.\
$c_h W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ at leading order.

{\bf What is actually reached.}  Annealing on algorithm 0 (\S\ref{sec:ip_numerics}; 7 seeds of $90000$
steps per kernel, agreeing to $3 \times 10^{-4}$) finds at most $1 + 0.050\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau = 1.025$
(box32), $1 + 0.056\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau = 1.051$ (w64) and $1.0003$ (p0): an irregular sparse comb of
$1$--$6$-sample valid runs (valid fraction $0.3$--$0.5$) within $\sim 1.5\tau$ of the kernel, a fully
masked annulus from $\sim 2\tau$ to $\sim 6\tau$ where the smoother's autocorrelation $(h*h)/h$ exceeds 1,
and a valid patch of $\sim 0.9\tau$ beyond it (for p0 the annulus alone).  The comb alone gives $1.022$
(box32) and $1.045$ (w64) in a sweep over periods $2$--$128$ and densities $1/8$--$7/8$ (the maximum is at
$n = 3/8$); the homogenized-smoother formula $1 + 0.442\,n(1 - n^{1/4})\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ of
\S\ref{sec:ip_lower0} ($\le 1 + 0.036\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ at $n = 0.41$, i.e.\ $1.018$/$1.032$) is
$20$--$40\%$ low at $\tau = 64$, where it was good to $10^{-4}$ at $\tau = 512$; the annulus adds the rest
(for box32 the near part alone gives $1.022$, the annulus alone $1.006$).  With enlargement switched on,
algorithm C reaches $1.12$ (box32) and $1.19$ (w64) on the structured run and edge masks of
\S\ref{sec:ip_bterm}, inside the theorem; a dedicated maximization (\S\ref{sec:ip_fom}) finds larger
values at small $\mu$, $1.083$ / $1.18$ at $\mu = 2$ and $1.136$ / $1.28$ at $\mu = 1.2$ for box32 / w64 (a
short kept run inside the support with everything within $3$--$4\tau$ enlarged), still inside the bound.

\subsection{Plain inpainting: the lower side}
\label{sec:ip_lower0}

By (\ref{eq:ip_var0}), $V'_p \ge \|(I - M)(k - S_0k)\|^2$, and every deficit is in the first term
$\|R_mk\|^2 = \|Mk\|^2 - 2k^TL_mk + \|L_mk\|^2$: the masked first pass shrinks the data toward its
baseline, and the kernel loses whatever part of itself the baseline can follow.  Writing
$\lambda_k = k^TL_mk/\|Mk\|^2$ for the leverage on the kernel, $\|R_mk\|^2/\|Mk\|^2 = 1 - 2\lambda_k +
\nu_k$ with $\nu_k = \|L_mk\|^2/\|Mk\|^2 \le \lambda_k$, the kernel-level version of the residual variance
of \dtnotes{Outputs}.  With $\bar\lambda$ the per-sample leverage averaged under the kernel,
$\lambda_k = W_{\rm eff}\,\bar\lambda$ to $0.1\%$ for $W \le 8$ in every configuration examined; for
box32 $\lambda_k$ is $1$--$2\%$ below $W_{\rm eff}\bar\lambda$ and for w64 $5$--$9\%$ below, because
$L_m$ is no longer flat on the scale of the kernel ($0.35$--$0.7\,\ell$ wide).  The per-sample leverage
of \dtnotes{Outputs} is still the first-order quantity; what separates benign from harmful
configurations at equal leverage is the recovery term $\nu_k$, which is $0.68$--$0.90$ of $\lambda_k$ in
the configurations examined ($0.76$ in the unmasked interior for box32), far from the
$L_mk = \lambda k$ idealization ($\nu = \lambda^2$).  The rest of this subsection is numerical (exact
linear algebra at $\tau = 64$ unless stated).

{\bf Islands.}  Let the kernel's support be valid, with gaps $G_1 \le G_2$ on either side and valid data
beyond.  For $G \gg W$ the baseline inside a gap is a cubic (the smoother reproduces cubics), the penalty
$\rho\sum(\Delta^2 f)^2$ is a bending energy, and displacing the island by $\delta$ costs
$c\,\rho\,\delta^2/G_1^3$ with $c = 3(1 + G_1/G_2)^3$ ($24$ for equal gaps, the clamped beam with a central
load; $3$ for $G_2 \rightarrow \infty$, the cantilever).  An island of $A$ valid samples then has its mean
mode fitted with leverage
\begin{equation}
\lambda_A = \frac{A}{A + K}, \qquad K = 3\rho\Big(\frac{1}{G_1 + \ell} + \frac{1}{G_2 + \ell}\Big)^3 ,
\label{eq:ip_island}
\end{equation}
where the $+\ell$ is an empirical correction (the valid data clamp the baseline about $\ell$ inside
their edge, not at it) that makes the fitted $c$ a universal function of $G/\tau$: for $A = 1$ the fitted
values at $\tau = 64$ ($1.21$, $4.20$, $9.31$, $14.6$, $18.6$, $21.1$ at $G/\tau = 1, 2, \ldots, 32$) are
those at $\tau = 512$, and the law is exact.  For $A = 32$ and $64$ ($A/\tau = \tfrac12$, $1$) it
under-estimates the leverage by $10$--$20\%$ for $G \lesssim 8\tau$ (at $G = 2\tau$: $0.53$ exact against
$0.45$ for $A = 32$, $0.73$ against $0.62$ for $A = 64$; at $G = 4\tau$, $0.80$ against $0.77$ and $0.90$
against $0.87$) and is exact for $G \ge 16\tau$; the excess is the finite width of the island relative to
$\ell$ (the beam's point load is wrong), not the slope mode, whose leverage stays below $0.05$ for
$A = 32$ but reaches $0.15$--$0.41$ for $A = 64$ at $G \ge 16\tau$, where it matters for the w64 floor.
For the boxcar matched to the island, $L_mk \approx \lambda_A k$ would give $V_p \approx W(1 - \lambda_A)^2$
and $V'_p/U_p \approx (1 - \lambda_A)^2(1 + 0.442\,W/\tau) + c_h W/\tau$; at $\tau = 64$ this
underestimates $V'/U$ by $0.02$ ($A = 1$) to $0.18$ ($A = 64$) at $\lambda_A \approx \tfrac12$, since
$\nu_k = 0.34$ against $\lambda_k^2 = 0.25$ on the tuned 32-island.  Half the leverage is reached at
$G_{1/2} = (c\rho/A)^{1/3} - \ell$ for $A = 1$ ($647$ two-sided, $277$ one-sided; the formula gives $648$
and $279$) but not for $A = 32$: numerically $G_{1/2} = 117$ two-sided (formula $142$), and one-sided
about one sample (formula $26$), because the 32-kernel simply touching a free gap already has
$\lambda_A = 0.50$, so the one-sided island {\em is} the valid-side edge case below.  Two-sided scan
for box32 on its own support ($V'/U$): $0.930$, $0.802$, $0.560$, $0.515$, $0.225$, $0.140$, $0.130$,
$0.129$ at $G = 32$, $64$, $117$, $128$, $256$, $512$, $1024$, $2048$; for p0, $0.964$, $0.844$, $0.451$,
$0.279$, $0.066$, $0.007$ at $G = 128$, $256$, $512$, $647$, $1024$, $2048$.  As $G \rightarrow \infty$
the floor is the simulated-noise term: $0.129$ for box32 ($c_h W/\tau = 0.133$ is $2.4\%$ high, the
support part of $\|S_0k\|^2$ no longer being negligible), $0.033$ (box8), $0.066$ (box16), $0.0042$ for
p0 ($c_h/\tau$ to $0.1\%$).  For the tapered kernels the island absorbs only the constant part of the
kernel and the shoulders survive: the floor is $(\|k\|^2 - (\sum k)^2/W)/U_p + \mbox{($n'$-term)} =
0.159 + 0.212 = 0.371$ for w64 on its full support ($0.317$ for w48) --- but a worse structure exists:
leaving only the 32 unit-weight core samples valid, with the shoulders masked along with the gaps, gives
$0.295$ (found by annealing at $B \ge 512$; at $B \le 256$ the full-support island is still worse for the
kernel).  A kernel narrower than its island: the rule that it loses only the fraction $W/A$ of its
variance fails at $\tau = 64$ for $A \ge 48$, since an island wider than $\sim\tau$ supports many
low-leverage modes; box32 centred on an island of $A = 48$, $64$, $128$ samples (gaps $4096$) has
$V'/U = 0.54$, $0.73$, $0.97$ ($W/A$ rule: $0.45$, $0.59$, $0.79$), and exceeds 1 at $A = 8\tau$.  The
isolation length scales as $\tau^{4/3}A^{-1/3}$ rather than as $\tau$.

{\bf Sparse periodic masks} (valid runs $A$, gaps $B$, density $n = A/(A+B)$, period $\ll \tau$).
For modes smooth on the period, ${\cal N}_m \approx nI + \rho D^TD$: the masked smoother is the unmasked
one with $\tau_{\rm eff} = \tau n^{-1/4}$, and the per-sample leverage is $c_2 n^{-3/4}/\tau$.  A kernel on
one run has, to leading order, $V'/U = 1 - (W/\tau)[0.442(n^{-3/4} - 1) - 0.265(1 - n)]$; at $\tau = 64$
this is off by $0.02$--$0.08$ for $n \ge 1/5$ and meaningless below $n \approx 1/8$ (the exact values
saturate at the island floor $0.129$ while the formula goes negative).  Exactly, for $W = A = 32$:
$0.894$, $0.756$, $0.624$, $0.505$, $0.265$, $0.137$ at $n = \tfrac12$, $\tfrac13$, $\tfrac14$,
$\tfrac15$, $\tfrac18$, $\tfrac1{16}$; the deficit reaches $10\%$ already at $n = 0.51$ (every other
32-run masked) and $50\%$ at $n = 0.20$ (gaps of $2\tau$).  For w64 on 64-runs, $10\%$ at $n = 0.65$ and
$50\%$ at $n = 0.29$, floor $0.371$.  A kernel spanning many periods sees the comb regime,
$V'/U = 1 + 0.442\,n(1 - n^{1/4})W_{\rm eff}/\tau \ge 1$ at leading order: the excess is real for every
$W \ge 2$ (p0 is below 1, $0.9976$ at $n = \tfrac12$), but the formula is $20$--$40\%$ low, e.g.\ the
every-other-sample comb gives $1.021$ (box32), $1.043$ (w64), $1.0046$ (box8) against $1.018$, $1.032$,
$1.0044$.

{\bf Gap edges.}  The leverage at the edge of a semi-infinite gap is $3.96\,c_2/\tau$ at $\tau = 64$
($4c_2/\tau$ with a $1\%$ finite-$\tau$ correction) and the corresponding $\nu$ is $3.97\,c_h/\tau$; the
excess decays into the valid data over $2$--$4\tau$ ($50\%$ of its edge value at $0.34\tau$, $10\%$ at
$\tau$, $1\%$ at $3.2\tau$), and the profile in units of $\tau$ is the same to three digits as at
$\tau = 512$.  A 32-wide kernel at the edge covers leverage from $3.96$ down to $2.0\,c_2/\tau$, so its
kernel-averaged edge leverage is $2.9\,c_2/\tau$.  A kernel on the valid side, touching a free gap, has
$V'/U = 0.599$ (box32), $0.568$ (w64; $0.556$ at its worst position, 16 samples into the gap), $0.865$
(box8), $0.982$ (p0), and inpainting does not help since nothing under the kernel is masked; the
leading-order $1 - 1.1\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ is valid only for $W_{\rm eff}/\tau \lesssim 0.15$ ($0.863$
for box8) and would give $0.45$/$0.01$ for box32/w64.  The deficit recovers over $\sim\tau/2$ as the
kernel moves away from the edge ($0.60$, $0.72$, $0.82$, $0.93$, $1.001$ at distance $0$, $8$, $16$, $32$,
$64$ for box32), with a $-1\%$ dip at $2\tau$ from the annulus of \S\ref{sec:ip_upper}.  Edge leverage
for finite gaps, in units of $c_2/\tau$: $1.02$, $1.10$, $1.42$, $1.81$, $2.37$, $2.94$, $3.64$ at
$G = 4$, $16$, $64$, $128$, $256$, $512$, $2048$ (scale-invariant: the $\tau = 512$ values at $8\times$
the gap); box32 touching a gap of $G = 64$/$256$/$1024$ is at $0.943$/$0.780$/$0.656$.  A kernel exactly
filling a gap of its own width has $V = 0$ but $V'/U = 1 - \sum_{\rm valid}(S_0k)_s^2/U_p = 0.871$
(box32), $0.788$ (w64), $0.967$ (box8), $0.996$ (p0) (leading order $1 - c_h W_{\rm eff}/\tau$: $0.867$,
$0.761$): the simulated noise restores everything except the smoothing leakage.  A kernel at the edge of
a long gap, inside it, is at $0.935$ (box32) / $0.894$ (w64), and one width further in at $0.967$/$0.982$.

{\bf Adversarial search.}  Annealing over masks within $\pm 24\tau$ of the kernel with a bound $B$ on the
number of masked samples (\S\ref{sec:ip_numerics}) found nothing beyond these structures: for
$B \lesssim 1.5\tau$ ($2\tau$ for w64) the optimum is the gap of width $W$ (for w64 at $B = 32 < W$, a
sub-support gap over its 32 unit-weight core samples, $0.946$), beyond that the symmetric island with
gaps $B/2$ (one-sided or asymmetric islands and sparse periodic masks are always worse for the
adversary), and the optimum matches the best analytic candidate to $10^{-5}$ at every budget, except
that for w64 at $B \ge 512$ the annealer found the core-only island, $0.02$--$0.08$ below the
full-support island.  This is the table in \S\ref{sec:ip_results}, item 3.

\subsection{Algorithm C: the lower bound}
\label{sec:ip_lowerC}

{\bf Theorem.}  Let $\lambda_{\rm thr} = \mu c_2/\tau$, $k \ge 0$ a kernel with support ${\rm supp}$, and
${\cal A} = {\rm supp} \cap m_2$ the kept part of the support.  For every mask,
\begin{equation}
V'_p(t) \;\ge\; \min_{{\cal A} \subseteq {\rm supp}}
   \Big[\, \|k_{\cal A}\|^2 \big(1 - \lambda_{\rm thr}\,W_{\rm eff}(k_{\cal A})\big)_+^2
   + \sum_{s \in {\rm supp} \setminus {\cal A}} \big(k_s - h[0]\textstyle\sum k\big)^2 \Big]
   \;=:\; U_p\,{\rm LB}_p(\mu) ,
\label{eq:ip_LB_exact}
\end{equation}
where $k_{\cal A}$ is $k$ restricted to ${\cal A}$ and $W_{\rm eff}(k_{\cal A}) = (\sum k_{\cal A})^2/\|k_{\cal A}\|^2$.
For every one of the 16 kernels and every $\mu \ge 1$ the minimizer is ${\cal A} = {\rm supp}$ (by
enumeration at $\tau = 64$), and then
${\rm LB}_p(\mu) = \|k\|^2(1 - \mu c_2 W_{\rm eff}/\tau)_+^2/U_p$, whose expansion is (\ref{eq:ip_LB}).
Where $\mu c_2 W_{\rm eff}/\tau \ge 1$ the bound is identically zero: w64 for $\mu \ge 3.14$, w48 for
$\mu \ge 4.24$, box32 for $\mu \ge 5.66$.

{\em Proof.}  By (\ref{eq:ip_variances}), $V'_C = \|R_m k_{\cal A}\|^2 + \sum_{s \notin m_2}(k_s - (S_0k)_s)^2$,
since $M_2 k = k_{\cal A}$.
(i) For $v$ supported on valid samples, with $\lambda_v = v^TL_mv/\|v\|^2 \in [0,1]$ (lemma of
\S\ref{sec:ip_upper}) and Cauchy--Schwarz $(v^TL_mv)^2 \le \|v\|^2\|L_mv\|^2$,
\begin{equation}
\|R_mv\|^2 = \|v\|^2 - 2v^TL_mv + \|L_mv\|^2 \;\ge\; \|v\|^2(1 - \lambda_v)^2 ,
\end{equation}
and $(1 - \lambda)^2$ decreases on $[0,1]$.
(ii) $L_m$ is positive semidefinite, so $|(L_m)_{ss'}| \le \sqrt{\lambda_s\lambda_{s'}}$; for $v \ge 0$
supported on kept samples, all of which have $\lambda_s \le \lambda_{\rm thr}$,
$v^TL_mv \le \big(\sum_s v_s\sqrt{\lambda_s}\big)^2 \le \lambda_{\rm thr}(\sum_s v_s)^2$, i.e.\
$\lambda_v \le \lambda_{\rm thr}W_{\rm eff}(v)$.  With (i), $\|R_mk_{\cal A}\|^2 \ge \|k_{\cal A}\|^2(1 - \lambda_{\rm thr}W_{\rm eff}(k_{\cal A}))_+^2$.
(iii) For $s \in {\rm supp}\setminus{\cal A}$, $(S_0k)_s = \sum_i h[s-i]k_i \le h[0]\sum k \le 0.265 \le \tfrac12 \le k_s$
(the first inequality since $h$ is positive over the support; the numbers are those of w64, and there
is no slack for a wider kernel), and
$x \mapsto (k_s - x)^2$ decreases on $[0, k_s]$, so $(k_s - (S_0k)_s)^2 \ge (k_s - h[0]\sum k)^2$ (if the
negative lobes of $h$ make $(S_0k)_s < 0$ the term only grows).  Masked samples outside the support
contribute $\ge 0$.  Summing gives (\ref{eq:ip_LB_exact}).
(iv) For a boxcar and $a = |{\cal A}|$ the bracket is $g(a) = a(1 - \lambda_{\rm thr}a)^2 + (W - a)(1 - Wh[0])^2$,
with $g'' = -4\lambda_{\rm thr} + 6\lambda_{\rm thr}^2 a < 0$ for $a < 2/(3\lambda_{\rm thr}) = 2\tau/(3\mu c_2)$,
which covers $[0, W]$ for every boxcar at $\mu \le 3$ (at $\tau = 64$, $a < 40$ at $\mu = 3$); then the
minimum is at an endpoint, and $g(W) - g(0) = W[(1 - \mu Wh[0])^2 - (1 - Wh[0])^2] \le 0$ for $\mu \ge 1$.
Beyond that (box32 at $\mu \ge 3.8$: $a \ge 31$ at $\mu = 4$, $a \ge 21$ at $\mu = 6$) concavity fails on
part of the range, and direct enumeration over $a = 0, \ldots, W$ gives the same conclusion.  For the tapered kernels the
bracket depends only on how many unit- and half-weight samples are kept, and enumeration gives the same
conclusion for every kernel and every $\mu \ge 1$ (at $\mu = 1$ exactly, $g(0) = g(W)$ to
$3 \times 10^{-5}$).  $\square$

\subsection{Algorithm A: the $b$-term}
\label{sec:ip_bterm}

Write $a = M_2k$ and $b = (M - M_2)S_0k$, so $\tilde k = a + b$ in (\ref{eq:ip_variances}) and
\begin{equation}
V'_A - V'_C = \|R_m(a + b)\|^2 - \|R_ma\|^2 = 2a^TR_m^2b + \|R_mb\|^2 = -2a^T(2L_m - L_m^2)\,b + \|R_mb\|^2 ,
\label{eq:ip_bterm}
\end{equation}
using $R_m^2 = M - 2L_m + L_m^2$ on the valid subspace and $a^TMb = 0$ (disjoint supports).  Near the
diagonal $L_m > 0$, so the cross term is negative: the first-pass residuals of nearby valid samples are
anti-correlated, ${\rm Cov}(r_s, r_{s'}) = -2(L_m)_{ss'} + (L_m^2)_{ss'} < 0$, because the masked fit
removed their common local mean.  Algorithm A drops the data of the enlarged samples but keeps their
imprint on $\hat f$, so the second pass re-adds a smoothed copy of their residuals to the kernel's own
residual, with negative covariance.  With $b_s \approx h[0]\sum k$ on the enlarged samples within $\sim\ell$ of
the kernel, the cross term is of order $-(\sum k)^2 h[0] \times$ (fraction of the $L_m$ row mass on enlarged
samples), i.e.\ first order in $W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ and independent of $\mu$; numerically
$-0.5\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ when the kernel's run is enlarged on both sides ($-0.25$ for box32 from
$\mu = 1.1$ to $2$, $-0.21$ at $\mu = 3$, $-0.13$ at $\mu = 4$; $-0.34$ for w64 up to $\mu = 2$) and
$-0.25\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ for one side ($-0.12$ box32, $-0.18$ w64, at the new edge of a long gap):
independent of $\mu$ up to $\mu \approx 2$ and gone by $\mu = 4$, where the rule enlarges nothing beside a
long gap.  On the same masks $V'_C$ is {\em above} 1 by $6$--$19\%$ (the kept run's residual plus the
high-passed $n'$ on its enlarged neighbours), which is the upper-side remark of \S\ref{sec:ip_upper}; a
dedicated maximization finds larger values at small $\mu$, $1.083$ / $1.18$ at $\mu = 2$ and $1.136$ /
$1.28$ at $\mu = 1.2$ for box32 / w64 (\S\ref{sec:ip_fom}).
Per-sample leverage gives no handle on the $b$-term: $b$ lives on samples {\em above} threshold, and its
effect is a cross-covariance.  Consequences:
\begin{itemize}
\item A's worst case saturates as $\mu \rightarrow 1$ at $0.84$ (box32) and $0.79$ (w64) at $\mu = 1.2$,
i.e.\ $1 - 0.32$ and $1 - 0.23\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau$, limited by the comb-island below, while C is at the
gap-filling floor $0.871$/$0.789$; making the rule more aggressive stops helping below $\mu \approx 1.5$
while its cost keeps growing.  A does not cross below the exact-form ${\rm LB}(\mu)$ at any
$\mu \ge 1.2$ (margin $\ge 0.05$ for box32, $\ge 0.18$ for w64): the $b$-term is at full strength on its
worst masks, but their $a$-part $\|R_ma\|^2$ is far from the extremal value the bound assumes, and the
bound is much weaker than every found worst case.
\item The enlargement is counter-productive for a kernel on the kept run next to an enlarged zone
($V'_A$ is below the un-enlarged value at that position at every $\mu$; on the comb-island masks below
the un-enlarged algorithm gives $0.84$/$0.94$/$0.99$ against A's $0.84$/$0.79$/$0.70$ at
$\mu = 1.2$/$1.5$/$2$ for box32), while it is what fixes the kernels inside the zone; the net worst case
at a long-gap edge still improves ($0.628$ un-enlarged $\rightarrow$ $0.836$/$0.899$/$0.935$ at the new
edge for $\mu = 2$/$1.5$/$1.2$, box32).
\item The annealed worst cases for A at $\mu \le 2$ are all $b$-term configurations, and all
``comb-islands'': the support kept at exactly threshold leverage, surrounded over $1$--$4\tau$ by an
irregular above-threshold comb of $1$--$6$-sample valid runs that the rule enlarges entirely, so that
$m_2$ is a bare island inside a masked neighbourhood ($0.701$ at $\mu = 2$ for box32, where C on the same
mask gives $1.084$ and the un-enlarged algorithm $0.99$: the $b$-term, $-0.24 = -0.48\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau$,
is the whole effect).  Behind it at $\mu = 2$ are a kept run between two long gaps ($0.755$), the island
beside an above-threshold comb ($0.731$), the tuned island ($0.784$) and the first kept samples at the
new edge of a long gap ($0.836$).  At $\mu = 3$ the box32 optimum is a weak comb-island ($0.514$,
$b = -0.08$) while for w64 it is already the bare island ($b = 0$); at $\mu = 4$ both are bare islands
with nothing enlarged (box32: the tuned island, $0.313$; w64: a partial-support island keeping the core
and one shoulder, $0.360$), and A and C coincide.  For p0 the comb-island holds up to $\mu = 3$
($b = -0.009 \approx -0.6\,W_{\rm eff}/\tau$).
\end{itemize}
What the bound can say for A is only $\|R_m(a + b)\| \ge \|R_ma\| - \|b\|$ with $\|b\|^2 \le \|S_0k\|^2$, a loss
of order $\sqrt{W_{\rm eff}/\tau}$, which is useless.  For algorithm C the whole question disappears.

\subsection{Numerical methods and validation}
\label{sec:ip_numerics}

{\bf Exact evaluation.}  For a given mask, (\ref{eq:ip_variances}) needs ${\cal N}_m^{-1}$ applied to a
few vectors, and $\lambda[t]$ needs its diagonal.  ${\cal N}_m$ is pentadiagonal but ill-conditioned:
its largest eigenvalue is $16\rho$ and its smallest is set by the smoothest modes, giving a condition
number of $3 \times 10^8$ for benign masks and up to $10^{13}$ for a free end (the free modes of a long
masked tail) at $\tau = 64$, against $10^{12}$--$10^{16}$ at $\tau = 512$.  Dense algebra
($T \le 4521$) is adequate at $\tau = 64$ except for the free-end masks, where a dense LU loses
$\sim 10^{-5}$ in the masked tail; a plain banded Cholesky loses the smooth modes, three steps of
iterative refinement recover them, and the refined banded solver agrees with an information-form
two-filter smoother (the forward and backward recursions of \dtnotes{Time detrending algorithm 2: Kalman
filter}, run over the whole timestream) to $9 \times 10^{-14}$ on every mask, including the free ends
(and to $10^{-6}$ with $30000$-sample gaps at $\tau = 512$).  The banded solver with refinement was used
everywhere (annealing, exact tables, Monte Carlo), the information-form smoother for the leverage
diagonal $\lambda[t] = ((J_f + J_b)^{-1})_{00}$ and as the cross-check of every annealed optimum
(agreement $10^{-6}$), and dense algebra for the eigenvalues and the solver cross-checks.

{\bf Adversarial search.}  Simulated annealing on $\pm\log(V'/U)$ over masks in a window of half-width
$24\tau$ around the kernel (valid clamps of $8\ell$ beyond), with single-sample flips, masking or
unmasking of random intervals, count-preserving transfers, boundary shifts and imposed periodic patterns,
optionally under a budget on the number of masked samples, followed by a deterministic single-flip
descent.  For algorithm 0, $7$--$15$ seeds per problem including the analytic candidates,
$60000$--$90000$ steps, $353$ runs; for A and C, with the rule inside the loop, $11$ seeds per problem
(including the algorithm-0 worst masks), $40000$ steps plus a two-pass descent over the whole window,
$776$ runs over the unconstrained and budgeted problems.  Everything was run natively at $\tau = 64$ with
the real kernels.  At fixed $W/\tau$ every length in the problem scales with $\tau$ except the island
isolation length ($\tau^{4/3}$), so a search at another $\tau$ with the same $W/\tau$ would transfer;
but the problem depends on $W/\tau$, and nothing was transplanted from the $\tau = 512$ baseline
($W/\tau = 1/16$) or from narrower kernels.  Every reported optimum is locally optimal under all single
flips.  Annealing cannot certify a global optimum; the evidence is the agreement of independent seeds
and the fact that no run ever went below the best analytic candidate by more than the amounts stated in
\S\ref{sec:ip_lower0} and \S\ref{sec:ip_lowerC}.

{\bf Monte Carlo.}  The literal two-pass procedure (masked smoother, inpainting, unmasked smoother,
then the 16 convolutions; nothing uses the identity or the variance formulas) was simulated at
$\tau = 64$ with $10^5$--$6 \times 10^5$ noise realizations per mask for eleven masks covering every
family above (gap $= W$, free and finite long-gap edges, the $G_{1/2}$ island, the adversarial island, a
sparse periodic mask, the comb, and the two annealed maximizing masks), all 16 kernels at every position
of a $\pm 256$ scan, giving $143632$ (kernel, position) comparisons with the exact formulas: pull r.m.s.\
$0.93$--$1.05$, maximum $|{\rm pull}|$ $4.7$, $443$ beyond $3\sigma$ against $388$ expected (the two
short-gap masks have slightly heavy tails), mean pull $-0.05$ from the shared realizations.  The comb
excess above 1 was resolved at $61\sigma$ (box32: $1.02100 \pm 0.00034$) and $89\sigma$ (w64), and for
every kernel of width $\ge 2$, while p0 is below 1 ($0.99752 \pm 0.00010$); the annealed maxima were
confirmed at $12\sigma$ (box32) and $25\sigma$ (w64) above 1.  For the enlarged algorithms the literal
three-pass procedure was simulated at $\tau = 64$ on the four worst masks at $\mu = 2$ ($2 \times 10^5$
realizations each; e.g.\ box32: $0.6996 \pm 0.0022$ vs $0.7009$ for A, $0.7797 \pm 0.0024$ vs $0.7805$
for C; w64: $0.5934 \pm 0.0018$ vs $0.5943$ and $0.6289 \pm 0.0020$ vs $0.6293$), $128$ (kernel, variant)
comparisons with pull r.m.s.\ $0.87$ and maximum $2.2$; among them $V'_C/U = 1.040$ for w64 on the
box32 comb-island mask, C above 1 at $12\sigma$.

\subsection{Remarks: algorithm B, fixed lag, renormalization}
\label{sec:ip_remarks}

{\bf Algorithm B} (re-run the first pass with $m_2$) has no $b$-term but its kept samples carry the
residual of the {\em smaller} mask, whose leverage is higher near the new edges and on any short kept run.
At the new edge of a long gap it reproduces the un-enlarged edge value ($0.628$ for box32) at every $\mu$,
because masking the edge just moves the $4\times$ edge leverage to the new edge; on a kept run between
two enlarged zones it turns the run into an island, at or near the island floors of
\S\ref{sec:ip_lower0}.  Iterating the rule to self-consistency does not help: for $\mu$ below the
long-gap edge leverage ($3.6$--$4$) each iteration creates a new above-threshold edge and the expansion
eats the whole timestream.

{\bf Fixed lag.}  With the production fixed-lag estimator ($L = 4\ell = 363$ at $\tau = 64$) the
identities (\ref{eq:ip_identities}) no longer hold exactly, since neither pass is a whole-timestream
solve.  An exact fixed-lag computation (an information-form engine validated against the python reference
{\tt KalmanDetrender}) shows that for algorithm 0 nothing moves by more than $3 \times 10^{-3}$: the
free-edge values are reproduced to $2 \times 10^{-4}$, the 32-island at $G_{1/2}$ (gaps $117 \ll L$,
with $2.7\,\ell$ of valid data inside the lookahead beyond them) moves from $0.560$ to $0.558$, and a
finite gap longer than $L$ ($G = 2048$) behaves as a free gap ($0.599$ instead of $0.628$), because the
far data no longer clamp the baseline on the lookahead side.  For algorithms A and C the rule is
sensitive to which leverage it uses: on the whole-stream worst masks at $\mu = 2$, tuned to the
threshold to $10^{-3}$, the fixed-lag leverage on the support is $0.1$--$4.6\%$ higher ($2.07$--$2.09$
against $2.00\,c_2/\tau$ on the comb-islands), so the fixed-lag rule drops part or all of the support
and the values jump (the box32 comb-island: A from $0.70$ to $1.006$, with the whole support inpainted).
These are not fixed-lag worst cases --- an adversary tuning to the fixed-lag leverage would rebuild the
same structures $1$--$5\%$ more isolated.  The conclusions of this section are qualitative in that
setting; the bound (\ref{eq:ip_LB_exact}) is not.

{\bf Renormalization.}  $V'_p(t)$ is a deterministic function of the mask, so the principled route to a
guarantee near 1 is not to mask more but to compute it and divide: the kernel-level
$1 - 2\lambda_k + \nu_k$ needs the $W$-band of ${\cal N}_m^{-1}$ and of ${\cal N}_m^{-1}M{\cal N}_m^{-1}$
(the $\nu$ of \dtnotes{Outputs} extended to lags up to $W$), roughly $O(W)$ $2 \times 2$ operations per
sample on top of the filter.  This has not been pursued.

\subsection{Dependence on $\tau$}
\label{sec:ip_tau}

The problem depends on the kernel and the smoother only through $W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ (and $A/\tau$ for an
island of $A$ valid samples).  At fixed $W/\tau$ every length scales with $\tau$ --- the edge-leverage
profiles, the expansion radii of \S\ref{sec:ip_overmask} and the comb structures at $\tau = 64$ are the
$\tau = 512$ ones at one eighth of the distance, and the fitted island constant $c$ of
(\ref{eq:ip_island}) is the same function of $G/\tau$ --- except the island isolation length, which
scales as $\tau^{4/3}A^{-1/3}$ ($G_{1/2} = 647$ for $A = 1$ at $\tau = 64$ against $11048$ at
$\tau = 512$, a factor $17 = 8^{4/3}$).  The previous baseline of these notes was $\tau = 512$ with the
same 16 kernels ($W_{\rm eff}/\tau = 1/16$ for box32), where the headline numbers were: plain-inpainting
worst cases $0.984$ (kernel filling a gap of its own width) and $0.929$ (kernel beside a long gap) for
box32, floors $0.017$ (box32) and $0.135$ (w64) reached at isolation gaps of $\sim 2900$ samples; worst
found for A and C at $\mu = 2$ of $0.957$ and $0.975$ (box32); largest excess above 1 of $1.0027$
(box32).  The $\tau = 64$ deficits are not those numbers times $8$: for box32 the valid-side edge deficit
goes from $0.071$ to $0.401$ ($5.6\times$), the gap $= W$ deficit from $0.0165$ to $0.129$ ($7.8\times$)
and the comb excess from $0.0023$ to $0.021$ ($9\times$), because the leading-order regime, in which every
deficit is a fixed multiple of $W_{\rm eff}/\tau$, ends around $W_{\rm eff}/\tau \approx 0.15$ (box8 at
$\tau = 64$ is still inside it: the linear edge formula gives $0.863$ against $0.865$ exact).  In units of
$W_{\rm eff}/\tau$ the worst-case deficit coefficients of Table \ref{tab:ip_summary} survive from
$\tau = 512$ to $\tau = 64$ for box32 to $10$--$20\%$ (for C, exactly), but not for w64, whose deficit
saturates at its core-island floor; and the exact-form bounds of \S\ref{sec:ip_upper} and
\S\ref{sec:ip_lowerC}, which depend on the kernel only through $\lambda_{\rm thr}W_{\rm eff}$ and
$h[0]\sum k$, transfer between a kernel at one $\tau$ and the kernel with $W_{\rm eff}$ scaled at the
other.

\subsection{The windowed figure of merit}
\label{sec:ip_fom}

This subsection contains the details behind \S\ref{ssec:weaker_fom}: the evaluation of the window
average, the searches for $X_{\rm hi}$ and $\tilde X_{\rm lo}$, the full per-kernel tables, the $T_c$
dependence, the Monte Carlo checks and the caveats.  Everything is at $\tau = 64$ with $T_c = 1024$, in
the setting of \S\ref{sec:ip_setup}.  Labels are as in \S\ref{sec:ip_results}: ``exact'' means exact
linear algebra for the mask named; ``exact scan'' the maximum over a finite family of masks, each
evaluated exactly; ``annealing'' or ``best found'' a value found by simulated annealing, exact for the
mask exhibited but not certified as the maximum; ``Monte Carlo'' a simulation of the literal procedure.

{\bf The windowed evaluator.}  $\tilde X_{\rm lo}$ needs $V'_p(t)$ at every position of a window
$[t_0, t_0 + T_c)$ for every kernel, $16 \times 1024$ quadratic forms per mask.  For algorithm 0, let
$S = [t_0 - 32, t_0 + T_c + 32)$ be the samples any kernel in the window touches and
$Z = {\cal N}_m^{-1}ME_S$ (one banded solve with $|S|$ right-hand sides; the masked columns are zero and
are skipped).  The $S$-block of $R_m^2 = M - 2L_m + L_m^2$ is $M_S - 2\,{\rm diag}(m_S)Z_S + Z^TMZ$, of
which only the band $|s - s'| < W_{\rm max}$ is needed, since it is contracted with $kk^T$; the band of
$Z^TMZ$ is computed blockwise (cost $T \times |S| \times 128$ instead of $T \times |S|^2$), and
$\|R_mk_{p,t}\|^2$ at every $t$ is a strided quadratic form on the band.  The $n'$-term
$\|(I - M)(k - S_0k)\|^2$ is the correlation of $1 - m$ with the squared impulse response of
$(I - S_0)$ applied to $k$ (kept to $\pm 2560$ samples, $e^{-2560/\ell} \sim 5 \times 10^{-13}$).  For A
and C the vectors $M_2k_{p,t}$ and, for A, $(M - M_2)S_0k_{p,t}$ are formed for every $t$ in the window
and solved in one banded solve with $T_c$ right-hand sides per (kernel, variant), using
$\|R_mv\|^2 = \|v\|^2 - 2v^Tw + \sum_s m_sw_s^2$ with $w = {\cal N}_m^{-1}v$ for $v$ supported on valid
samples; the leverage $\lambda[t]$ that the rule needs at every $t$ comes from the banded Cholesky
factor by the Takahashi recursion (the band of ${\cal N}_m^{-1}$ from the factor, $O(T)$).  The banded
Cholesky with iterative refinement of \S\ref{sec:ip_numerics} is used throughout; in the clamped
geometry below the refinement changes nothing ($4 \times 10^{-10}$).  Cost at $T = 3072$: $0.1$--$0.2$ s
per mask for all 16 kernels (algorithm 0), $0.1$ s per (mask, kernel, variant) for A and C.  A pointwise
evaluator (one or two right-hand sides, $\sim 1$ ms) drives the $X_{\rm hi}$ annealing.

{\em Validation.}  The evaluator reproduces every reference number of
\S\ref{sec:ip_constants}--\S\ref{sec:ip_overmask} to the digits given there: the constants of the
smoother, $U_p$ (Table \ref{tab:ip_Up}), the gap $= W$, free-edge, finite-gap-edge, island, comb and
sparse-periodic values of \S\ref{sec:ip_lower0} (e.g.\ the two-sided island scan $0.930 \ldots 0.129$,
including $0.560$ at $G = 117$), $B_{1,p}/U_p$, the isolated-gap expansion table of \S\ref{sec:ip_overmask}
entry by entry, the density threshold, the tuned islands $0.784$/$0.527$/$0.315$ and $0.634$/$0.394$/$0.371$
of \S\ref{sec:ip_lowerC}, the new-edge values $0.835$/$0.900$/$0.936$ of \S\ref{sec:ip_bterm}, and Table
\ref{tab:ip_summary} to $0.001$--$0.006$ for $28$ of its $30$ entries (the two C entries at $\mu = 1.2$
were seed-limited by $0.005$/$0.015$ in the re-run).  Against dense algebra (dense ${\cal N}_m^{-1}$, $R_m$
and a finite-$T$ $S_0$ at $T = 2048$ on structured random masks) the windowed evaluator agrees to
$1.2 \times 10^{-8}$ (algorithm 0, 16 kernels at 29 positions; the dense solve's own rounding at
$\kappa \sim 10^8$), $2.4 \times 10^{-7}$ (A) and $4.6 \times 10^{-8}$ (C), the Takahashi leverage to
$8 \times 10^{-9}$, and the pointwise and windowed evaluators agree to $2 \times 10^{-10}$; $m \equiv 1$
gives $V' = U$ at every position to $6 \times 10^{-11}$, $m \equiv 0$ to $8 \times 10^{-15}$, and at
$\mu = \infty$ A $=$ C $=$ algorithm 0 to $4 \times 10^{-10}$.  Monte Carlo of the literal procedure on one
structured mask, $32000$ realizations, all 16 kernels at all 1024 window positions: pull r.m.s.\ $1.011$
and maximum $|{\rm pull}|$ $3.9$ over $16384$ (kernel, position) pairs for algorithm 0, and $1.02$ / $4.65$
for each of A and C at $\mu = 2$.

{\em Geometry.}  For $\tilde X_{\rm lo}$ the window is $[1024, 2048)$ of a $T = 3072$ stream; the mask is
free on the window plus $8\tau = 512$ margins on each side ($2048$ free samples) and all valid beyond

For $X_{\rm hi}$ the mask is free within $\pm 24\tau$ of the kernel for algorithm 0 (as in

$\sim 6\tau$, and an enlarged sample beyond $8\tau$ enters only through the $b$-term, at
$< 3 \times 10^{-3}$ relative), with valid clamps of $8\ell$ beyond.

the rule (the reference numbers above, the dense algebra, $V' = U$ for $m \equiv 1$, and, directly from

enlarged window contributes nothing to $\tilde X_{\rm lo}$); the scan values (exact for every mask on
the grid, phase maximized exactly); the window averages of the ten Monte Carlo masks.  Best found, not
certified: $X_{\rm hi}$ (algorithm 0: 4 seeds to $6 \times 10^{-4}$, reproducing the 7-seed optima; A, and C
